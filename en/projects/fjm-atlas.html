Commissioned project / interactive literary atlas / 2026

FJM Atlas /
Interactive Map

For the exhibition Manuscrit trouvé à Saragosse | enquête sur un roman-monde, the atlas turns the geography of Jean Potocki’s novel into a tactile editorial interface: visitors move between places, stories and sources while keeping the feeling of one continuous literary investigation.

Status commissioned / in development / not yet deliveredRole interaction design / software architecture / development / map integrationSystem Electron / TypeScript / OpenSeadragon / offline runtime
01

Context + brief

Make a “world-novel” explorable without reducing it to a conventional map.

The exhibition investigates Jean Potocki’s The Manuscript Found in Saragossa and the circulation of its nested stories across places and periods. The device must let visitors explore those locations, understand their links to stories and characters, and move between them without losing either reading continuity or the documentary nature of the exhibition.

02

Response + system

A museum interface built around the map, not around menus.

I designed and developed the atlas as a dedicated offline kiosk application. The high-resolution tiled map remains the main object; touch navigation, location selection, editorial reading, related places and idle behaviour form one continuous system. Cartography, interaction logic and editorial content remain separated so the map, gestures and texts can evolve independently.

high-resolution map→touch navigation→interactive locations→editorial cartels→related places→offline kiosk

Electron / TypeScript / OpenSeadragon / tiled-map pipeline / pan + pinch + double-tap / structured editorial content / attract mode / inactivity reset / kiosk packaging / telemetry + remote maintenance

03

Interface

From world map to editorial reading.

A location selection keeps the map in view while opening its editorial material. The interface is designed so exploration can move between geography, text and related places without breaking the visitor’s spatial context.
